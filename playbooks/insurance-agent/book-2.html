Chapter 5

Turn quote data
into a clear story.

Clients rarely need more information. They need help understanding what matters, what changed and what they should compare.

Bad comparison

Compare premium only. Ignore different deductibles, limits and endorsements.

VS
Better comparison

Compare meaningful differences and explain tradeoffs in plain language.

Never let AI invent policy facts.

Use it to organize verified carrier information—not generate policy language from memory.

Follow Up Without
Chasing

A good follow-up adds value. A bad follow-up only asks whether they decided yet.

12
Follow-Up Timeline

Every touch has
a job.

DAY 0
Recap

Confirm what was discussed, what matters and the agreed next step.

DAY 2
Clarify

Address one likely question or comparison point.

DAY 5
Reframe

Reconnect the recommendation to the client's priority.

DAY 10
Decision

Ask whether timing, fit or another issue is preventing movement.

DAY 21
Close loop

Offer a clean re-entry point instead of endless “checking in.”

Chapter 7

Diagnose the objection
before answering it.

“Too expensive” may mean price. It may also mean confusion, low urgency, a bad comparison or uncertainty about the value.

If they say...

“This is more than I expected.”

ASK
Clarify...

“Is the concern the total premium, the monthly payment, or how it compares with another option?”

PRICE

Compare correctly

Verify limits, deductibles, endorsements and exclusions before declaring one option cheaper.

TRUST

Reduce uncertainty

Slow down and clarify what is unclear instead of piling on more sales language.
